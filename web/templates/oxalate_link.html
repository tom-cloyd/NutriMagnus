Reading a reference entry {{ manual_link("oxalate-row", "Learn more") }}

An entry in the Harvard table (433 foods) looks like this:

Spinach, Raw — 316.2 mg  / 1 cup  [very high]
Vegetables · measured in this food
  • 316.2 mg / 1 cup — oxalate per the serving the table used, not per 100 g. Where the serving is a weight (ounces), NuMa also shows = x mg/100g and can scale it to any amount you eat. Where it's a cup, piece or tablespoon, there is no per-100 g figure, and that food is left out of recipe oxalate totals rather than guessed at.
  • [very high] — the band NuMa colours the value with. From a per-100 g figure: very high 300 mg or more, high 100+, moderate 25+, low 5+, negligible below that. Rows that have only a per-serving figure are banded on that instead, at 100 / 26 / 10 / 2 mg. {{ manual_link("oxalate-categories", "Learn more") }}
  • Vegetables — the table's own food group, useful when two entries have near-identical names.
  • measured in this food — the table marks which values were measured directly and which were estimated from a similar food. An estimated value is still worth having, but it is an inference, not a measurement. {{ manual_link("oxalate-measured", "Learn more") }}

What NuMa saves: your choice of entry, marked as confirmed by you — so the warning stops and the name-matching never runs for this food again. Choosing not in the reference table is saved the same way, and means this food contributes no oxalate figure at all rather than an inaccurate one. {{ manual_link("oxalate-what-is-stored", "Learn more") }}
