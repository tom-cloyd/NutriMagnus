8. Glycemic Load — glycemic load and estimated GI for foods, meals, recipes and days

With this on, NuMa shows glycemic load (GL) everywhere it applies: on food, meal, recipe and day pages (with an estimated GI for meals, recipes and days), the day’s GL so far under a meal’s and a day’s title, the GL columns and sort on Meals & Log and Recent Days, the GL trend and plot series, Compare, What-if, Top Contributors’ GL ranking and lower-GL swaps, printouts, and your daily GL target. Turning it off hides all of that. GI values are still shown and kept, and nothing is deleted — turning it back on brings everything back, including a daily target you’ve set. {# ── 9. Water tracking ────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
9. Water Tracking — water from drinks and from food, against a daily goal

With this on, meal, recipe and day pages show a Water row split into water from drinks and water from food. Foods and recipes get a “This is a drink” tick-box, and NuMa flags a food whose name says it’s dry (a flour or powder, say) but whose data says it’s mostly water — the sign of a wet food picked in place of the dry one. Turning it off hides all of this; no water values are deleted, so turning it back on brings everything back. The goals are the US Institute of Medicine’s: total water 3.7 L a day for men and 2.7 L for women, of which drinks 3.0 and 2.2 L. From age 65, a day’s page also warns when its drinks come to less than the minimum the European Society for Clinical Nutrition and Metabolism (ESPEN) recommends for older people (2.0 L men, 1.6 L women). {{ manual_link("water-older") }}
